The job

What the tool is for

Your team needs client companies. Someone exports a list of prospects from Apollo, loads it in, and the console handles everything after that: it researches each company, writes a personal cold email, sends it, chases it up if nobody replies, watches your inbox for responses, and tells you which replies look promising.

The point is that a team member should never have to write a cold email by hand, and should never have to remember to follow up.

The data

The things it keeps track of

Seven kinds of record. Everything the tool does is reading or writing one of these.

CLIENT

A prospect

One person at one company — their name, email, job title, industry, website. Carries a status that moves through Not Contacted → Pinged → Follow Up → Replied → Meeting Set, plus how many times you've chased them and how their last reply felt.

TEAMMEMBER

One of your people

Extends a normal login with their role, the name that signs their emails, and their Gmail address and app password. The password is encrypted before it touches the database.

OUTREACHCAMPAIGN

A campaign

An optional named template that steers the tone of a batch. If you don't pick one, the built-in default is used.

ACTIONLOG

A record of one sent email

Who sent it, to whom, the full text that went out, whether Gemini wrote it or the plain template did — and now its quality score and any defects found.

EMAILREPLY

A reply that came back

The message found in your inbox, plus Gemini's read on it: Positive, Interested, Neutral, Negative, or Not Interested.

COMPANYRESEARCH

What we learned about a company

The Google research summary, saved so it isn't looked up again for a month. Also records whether the search actually found anything.

CAMPAIGNRUN · FOLLOWUPRUN · SCANRUN

A batch in progress

Live progress for one background job — how far through it is, which company it's on, what happened to each contact. This is what the progress bar reads.

The engine

Three jobs run in the background

None of these happen while you wait on a page. You click, the work is handed to a background worker, and you watch a progress bar.

JobWhat it doesRuns on its own
Send campaign Finds every contact marked Not Contacted that belongs to you or is unclaimed, writes each a personal email, sends it, marks them Pinged. No — you start it
Send follow-ups Finds people who were emailed but never replied, and chases them. Up to three times, at 3 days, then 7, then 14. Tone softens each round; the third is a polite last attempt. Every 6 hours
Scan for replies Logs into your Gmail, looks through the last 90 days for replies from anyone you've contacted, saves them, has Gemini judge the sentiment, and flips that contact to Replied. Every 2 hours
Tidy up Catches batches that died halfway — a server restart mid-send — and marks them failed instead of leaving them stuck as "running" forever. Every 15 minutes

Not switched on yet

The automatic schedules above are written and ready, but they need a background worker process running on your host. There's no Procfile in the repo and no worker configured, so right now the three jobs only fire when someone clicks the button. Turning this on needs a host decision, a Redis instance, and a nominated mailbox for the unattended runs to send from.

The core

What happens to one email

This is the sequence every single outbound email goes through. Stages 4 and 5 are new.

A good email takes the top path and nothing has changed for it. Only a defective draft costs the extra Gemini call, and only a twice-defective one falls back to the plain template. Either way the outcome is written down.

Claim the contact existing

The contact is locked before any work starts. If two people run a campaign at the same moment, or someone double-clicks, only one run can grab a given contact — so nobody gets emailed twice. If the send later fails, the lock is released and they're eligible again next time.

Research the company changed

Gemini searches Google for what the company does, what they've launched, any recent news. This used to run once per person. It now runs once per company and the result is kept for 30 days, so two contacts at the same firm cost one search instead of two, and a re-run costs nothing. If the search finds nothing, that's recorded rather than quietly ignored — and it's retried in a few hours rather than being stuck for a month.

Gemini writes the email existing

Everything known about the contact plus the research goes into a prompt with a sample email to follow. Gemini replies in a fixed format with a subject and a body, so nothing has to be guessed at from raw text. If Gemini is rate-limited or its servers are busy, it's retried up to four times with increasing waits. If the request was rejected outright, it fails immediately rather than wasting retries.

Check the email is fit to send new

A proofreader step. It scores the draft out of 100 and looks for the things that make an email unsendable: leftover blanks like [Company] or [specific domain], template codes like {{first_name}}, Gemini talking to you instead of the prospect ("Here is the email you requested:"), an email that never names the company, a missing signature, a draft cut off mid-sentence, the wrong person's name in the greeting. This is plain pattern-matching, not another AI call, so it's instant and free.

Give it one chance to fix itself new

A failed draft isn't thrown away. Gemini gets its own rejected email back with a list of exactly what's wrong and is asked to correct it. If the second attempt passes, it's sent and flagged as repaired. If it fails again, your plain template is sent instead — still a professional email, just not personalised — and the failure is recorded honestly instead of being counted as a success.

Send it existing

Before a batch starts, the tool logs into your Gmail once to confirm the password works — so a wrong password fails immediately instead of after burning Gemini credits on all fifty contacts. One connection is then reused for the whole batch, with a 30-second cutoff so a stalled connection can't hang forever, and a 2-second gap between sends so Gmail doesn't throttle you.

Write down what happened expanded

The contact flips to Pinged and a log entry is saved: the full email text, whether Gemini or the template wrote it, and now the quality score, the specific defects found, whether it needed repairing, and whether real research was behind it. This is what the reliability page reads.

Worth knowing

Three honest caveats

The schedules aren't live

Automatic follow-ups and reply scanning are written and tested, but need a background worker running on your host. Until that's set up, they only run when someone clicks.

The research cache saves nothing on your current list

Your Apollo export has 25 contacts across 25 different companies, so there's nothing to skip on a first run. The saving shows up on re-runs, and on lists with several people at the same firm.

Nobody knows the real repair rate yet

The repair step is proven to work, but how often Gemini actually fixes its own draft can only be measured on a live batch. Run one real campaign and the reliability page will show the true split between repaired and blocked.
